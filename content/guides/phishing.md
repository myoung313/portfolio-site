title: How to spot a phishing email
summary: The warning signs that catch most phishing, and what to do if someone clicked.
---
## The warning signs
Most phishing emails share a few tells:
- Urgency or fear: "your account will be closed today", "invoice overdue".
- A sender name that looks right but an address that doesn't, like microsoft-support@outlook-verify.com.
- Links whose real address (hover or long-press to see it) doesn't match the company.
- Unexpected attachments, especially invoices, voicemails or shared documents you weren't expecting.
- Requests to change bank details, buy gift cards, or sign in to "verify" your account.

## Business email compromise
The costliest scams don't contain links at all. An attacker poses as your boss, a vendor or a client and asks for a payment or a change to bank details. Always confirm payment changes with a phone call to a number you already have, never one from the email.

## What to do if someone clicked
Act quickly and without blame, so people report mistakes fast:
1. Change the user's password and sign them out of all sessions (Microsoft 365 admin center, Users, Active users, select the user).
2. Check that MFA is on for that account.
3. Look for new inbox rules that forward or delete mail, a common attacker trick (see the Suspicious inbox rules guide).
4. Run an antivirus scan if a file was opened.
5. Report the email with the Report button in Outlook so Microsoft's filters learn from it.
