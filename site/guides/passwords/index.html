Passwords and password managers for small teams

Long passphrases, a team password manager, and no forced 90-day changes.

What a good password looks like now

Length beats complexity. A passphrase of four or more random words is stronger and easier to remember than a short jumble of symbols. Never reuse a work password anywhere else. Current NIST guidance says not to force regular password changes, and to change a password only when there's a sign it was exposed.

Use a team password manager

A password manager creates and stores a unique password for every site, fills it in automatically, and lets you share logins safely with coworkers instead of using spreadsheets or sticky notes. Choose one with business features: shared vaults, admin recovery, and MFA on the vault itself.

Check for leaked passwords

Check whether a password already appears in known breaches with the free CloudShift Labs Password breach check at cloudshiftlabs.net/password. It checks safely and never sends your password. In Microsoft 365, Entra ID Password Protection blocks common weak passwords automatically.

Passwordless is the future

Passkeys and the Microsoft Authenticator app can replace passwords entirely for Microsoft 365 sign-ins. They can't be phished the way passwords can, so they are worth piloting once MFA is in place.
